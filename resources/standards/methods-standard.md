# Methods and statistics standard

## Design
- The unit of analysis matches the unit of randomisation or sampling (animals vs pens vs barns; plants vs plots; images vs cameras). Treating repeated or nested observations as independent is critical.
- Appropriate controls or comparators exist; their absence is justified.
- Allocation is randomised or the selection mechanism is described; blinding is described where outcome assessment is subjective.
- Known confounders are measured, controlled or discussed.

## Measurement
- Instruments, protocols and calibration are described; validity and reliability of key measures are reported or cited.
- For annotated data: who annotated, with what protocol, and inter-annotator agreement.

## Sample size and inference
- Sample size is justified (power, precision, or practical constraint stated honestly).
- The primary outcome and analysis were fixed before seeing results, or the deviation is disclosed.
- Multiple comparisons are handled.
- Model assumptions are checked; violations are addressed.
- Effect sizes come with uncertainty (CI, SE, credible interval), not only p-values.
- Statistical significance is not confused with practical relevance.

## Severity guide
Wrong unit of analysis, missing comparator for a causal claim, or analysis chosen after seeing outcomes without disclosure → critical. Missing uncertainty or unjustified sample size → major.
