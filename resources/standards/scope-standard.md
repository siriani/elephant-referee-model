# Scope standard

1. **Contribution statement.** A single, specific contribution is stated early. Vague contributions ("we propose a novel framework") without the difference from prior work are a major finding.
2. **Positioning.** The closest prior approaches are named and the difference is stated (method, data, setting, result). Missing the obvious standard alternative to the proposed approach is major.
3. **Novelty claims.** Claims of being "first" or "novel" are backed by the cited literature or removed.
4. **Fit to venue.** If a venue is given: audience, evidence type and length fit its scope. A methods paper with a single small case study, or a field study framed as a methods paper, is flagged.
5. **Scope of conclusions.** Conclusions do not generalise beyond the population, site, season, dataset, species or conditions studied without saying so.
6. **Focus.** Material that does not serve the main claim is identified for removal or supplementary material.
